# Taste and Context

## Project assignment

Explore how the context of eating shapes the experience of food, beginning with hunger and the satisfaction of a craving.

## Starting observation

“Today, I didn't have lunch, and the two oranges I ate were satisfying and fulfilled my cravings for food at the moment.”

This example suggests that the experience involved more than the oranges' flavor: eating them met an immediate need. It leaves an interesting question open: did the oranges taste better, feel more satisfying, or both?

## Guiding question

How does hunger influence the way I describe a food's taste and the satisfaction of eating it?

## Exploration

Observe a few ordinary occasions when you eat the same food, such as an orange. Let differences in hunger occur naturally; this assignment does not require skipping meals or changing how much you eat.

For each occasion, write a short entry describing:

- **Before eating:** How hungry were you? What were you craving or expecting?
- **Context:** Where were you, who were you with, and were you rushed or relaxed?
- **Taste:** What did you notice about sweetness, sourness, aroma, or texture?
- **Satisfaction:** How did eating the food meet your needs or expectations?
- **Reflection:** What seemed to matter most about this particular moment?

## Deliverable

Create three brief observation entries and a concluding paragraph comparing them. Distinguish descriptions of flavor from descriptions of hunger relief, enjoyment, and satisfaction. Note any differences in the food itself that could also explain the experience.

Use these observations to refine the guiding question before choosing a design or building a prototype. These personal observations can reveal patterns to explore, but cannot establish that hunger alone caused a change in taste.
